Working with pane display bars

Pane display bars are used when tool windows or configuration dialogs contain at least one panean entirely separate working area of a window, often separated by a pane display bar. See pane display bar.. These bars are located between two adjacent panes and can be used to do the following:

  • to increase the size of one pane by hiding the adjacent pane
  • to adjust the relative sizes of the two adjacent panes, like a splitter bar.
  • to display a pane which has been hidden 

Pane display bars are indicated by two arrows separated by a row of dots and are always positioned in the center of the left and/or right border of the pane that is able to be enlarged.

Note: When a pane display bar is situated between two panes, the border above and below this pane display bar becomes a splitter bar, therefore point above or below the display bar, until the mouse pointer assumes the splitter bar icon  and drag this bar to adjust the widths of these adjacent panes accordingly.

The direction in which the arrows point, indicate whether a pane will be hidden or displayed, as follows:

  1. If a pane display bar is between two adjacent panes, then the pane that the arrows point to will be hidden, when the pane display bar is clicked.

Tip: This is especially useful if you need more space to display values or to work within the pane.

  1. If a pane display bar is situated on the border of the window itself and the arrow is pointing in the direction of the window’s contents then a pane will be displayed, when the pane display bar is clicked.
