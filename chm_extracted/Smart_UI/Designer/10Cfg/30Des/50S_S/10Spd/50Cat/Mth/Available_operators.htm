Available operators

The expression string is a sequence of operators and their operandsthe term(s) or sub-expression(s) that an operator acts upon., which are evaluated and resolved to produce the Result output.

The operators follow a strict precedence, which define the order in which their operands are evaluated.

In addition to this, operators also act on either the operand on their left or the operand on their right, which is known as their "associativity". Click for more details:

The following table shows the precedence and associativity of operators (from highest to lowest precedence):

The first column of the table also specifies how the operator is to be used, and designates the operands, as follows:

  1. Primary: primitive members of expressions like constants, variables or functions.
  2. Terms: separable parts of expressions, and can be signed terms or the product, quotient or modulus of two signed terms.

Note1: Operators in the same segment of the table have equal precedence and are evaluated from left to right in an expression.

Note2: This order of precedence can be overridden through the use of brackets '('..')', to force some parts of an expression to be evaluated before others.

Operations within brackets are always performed before those outside. Although within the brackets operator precedence is maintained.

Note: The Associativity of Boolean operators is always from left to right with the exception of the Boolean inversion ! operator which is from right to left.

Since Boolean True is evaluated as 1 and Boolean False is evaluated as 0, the following equation:

((!i1&&i2)+(i2*20)), where i1 = 0 and i2 = 1, evaluates to: ((1)+(20)) = 21.

IMPORTANT: When adding multiple “&&” , “||” and other expressions, for instance: (i1<i2)&&(i1>10), each expression MUST be enclosed in parentheses “()”. If these parentheses are left out, these expressions do not evaluate correctly.
