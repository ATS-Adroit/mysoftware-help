What is maximum demand and why should you monitor it?

Maximum demand is an additional cost for most non-domestic power users. It is the method used by your electricity provider to control how much electricity you use, especially at peak times, when your usage coincides with peak domestic usage.

Your maximum demand limit is the maximum amount of electricity that you are allowed to use to run your process during peak demand times, which in South Africa is measured in kVAh. Both this amount and the cost of providing this power are agreed between you and your electricity provider, with the condition that if you exceed this amount then you will pay heavy penalties.

Your electricity provider monitors your maximum demand in “block” periods (in South Africa these are every 30 minutes) during these peak times. Your monthly electricity bill uses the highest recorded block kVA usage as the kVA for the whole month. In other words, at the end of the billing month you pay the penalty resulting from the largest divergence from your maximum demand for a block period, for the ENTIRE month.

For instance, if you exceed your maximum demand in the first block period of the month, you will pay this penalty for the entire month, even if you turn off your plant at this point! Needless to say, the cost penalties for the excess power are very high and this is why power management is so important.

Consequently monitoring your maximum demand can result in considerable savings and reduced power usage. However, it is recommended to automate this monitoring, such as by using an MaxDemand agent, because one mistake can easily exceed your maximum demand limit for the current billing period.
